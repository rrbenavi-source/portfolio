Eight generations, one job

Each product changed what question the business could ask without asking IT. None changed who decides what the number means.

Generation
Analysis it enables
What it doesn't allow
1
The bedsheetCOBOL · ALV · Crystal
Operational: list, filter, trace back to the document
Asking a new question without a request to IT
2
The spreadsheetVisiCalc 1979 · Excel 5 1993
Scenarios and pivot tables without code
Two people arriving at the same number
3
The cubeEssbase · SSAS · BW/BEx
Multidimensional: slice, drill down, year over year
Asking what wasn't modeled
4
The semantic layerBO universe 1991
Governed ad hoc with business objects
Maintaining itself: someone adds every object
5
Visual discoveryPolaris/Tableau 2002 · Qlik
Exploratory: look before you ask
A single truth: every workbook, its own math
6
Self-servicePower BI 2015 · Looker · SAC
Self-built models and shared dashboards
Controlling model sprawl
7
The first AI waveQ&A · Ask Data · Search to Insight
Automated diagnosis and keyword phrases
Conversation: synonyms captured by hand
8
The agentCopilot · Genie · Cortex · Joule · Pulse
Conversational and multi-step
Guaranteeing the same answer every time
The product changed eight times. The job of deciding what each business word means never went away.
Sources: Codd et al. (1993) · US Patent 5,555,403 · Stolte, Tang and Hanrahan (2002) · Microsoft, Tableau, SAP, Databricks and Snowflake documentation. Reference dates, not exhaustive.
